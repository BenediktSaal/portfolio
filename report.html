01

What goes in, what comes out

Input

One free-text message from a customer, as it arrives in the inbox. No form, no category, no priority — just a subject line and a paragraph.

Output

The same ticket with a route, the reason for it, and either a reply ready for a person to send, a question back to the customer, or a briefing for the case handler on what to check first.

03

Two of the twelve outputs

Ticket V-01 · in

“I can't log in to the customer portal anymore, my password isn't accepted. How do I reset it? Policy 4471-882-19.”

Out · auto reply

Dear Customer, thank you for reaching out regarding your login issue with policy 4471-882-19. To reset your password, please go to the customer portal login page and click on “Forgot password?”. You will receive an email with a link to set a new password. If you do not receive this email within a few minutes, please check your spam folder or contact us again so we can assist further. Best regards, Customer Service

Ticket V-08 · in

“I just got a confirmation that my bank details for the direct debit were changed yesterday. I did not change anything. The new IBAN is not mine.”

Out · human review

Suspected fraud: unauthorized bank/IBAN change on direct debit. Immediately verify identity of requester and check account change logs/audit trail for who initiated the change and from where. Freeze/reverse the direct debit change pending verification, alert fraud/security team, and contact the customer via a verified channel (not reply-only) to confirm details and secure the account. Do not confirm or deny any account details in writing until verified. High priority due to financial risk and potential unauthorized access.

04

What the run showed

A quarter of the inbox leaves without anyone writing. Three tickets came back finished: two answers and one question back to the customer. Everything with a contractual, financial or unresolved technical angle went to a person — including the claim status request and the failed photo upload, which a first-level team would often handle itself. That sets the realistic ceiling for a pilot: roughly a quarter of the volume, not half.

The safety net was needed once. The ticket that says nothing beyond “something isn't right” came back at 0.65 confidence and was moved to review by the rule, not by the agent. The risk screen never had to override a routing decision, because every fraud, refund and cancellation case had already been sent to a person. It costs nothing when the agent is right and catches the case where it is not.

One defect the run surfaced. The first attempt answered English tickets in German, because the brief described a German insurer. Harmless in a demo, wrong in production. Fixed by pinning the reply language; the run above is the corrected one.

Twelve synthetic insurance tickets, no real customer data. Both sample outputs are verbatim; the V-08 briefing is from the preceding run of the same set, where it was routed and scored identically.
