Agent workflow

Service ticket triage

Customer service inboxes are mostly routine with a few cases that are anything but. This agent reads every incoming message, decides whether it can be answered, and writes the reply. This way the team confirms and sends instead of composing from scratch. Anything sensitive is taken out of the automated path and handed to a person, together with an evaluation report. 12 exemplary service tickets were used for the test run.

Where this came from

Working alongside service teams, it became clear that routine tickets are taking most of the time, while the critical cases don't always get the attention they deserve.

Read the run report One complete run · 12 tickets · every decision listed
Situation
Service inbox with 100% manual processing.
What it does
Reads each message, sorts it, writes the reply or the briefing for a person
Effect
Ticket replies are ready to send, escalated or sorted out.
01

The problem

  • Everything lands in one inbox as free text, so the priority of a request only becomes visible once somebody has read the whole thing
  • Experienced case handlers spend the day on questions a standard answer would cover
  • The cases that need a fast reaction like suspected fraud, a changed bank account, a cancellation are found late, often only after the customer writes a second time
02

How it works

In
A free-text message
Whatever the customer wrote. No form, no category, no priority.
Step 1
Sorted into one of four
Answer it, ask for what is missing, hand it to a person, or set it aside as spam.
Step 2
Checked against fixed rules
Fraud, payment changes, cancellations, refund disputes, legal threats and injury always go to a person.
Out
A reply or a briefing
A draft ready to send, a question back to the customer, or what the case handler should check first.
03

What it changes

Measured on a full run: 3 of 12 tickets came back finished, 2 answers and 1 question back to the customer. Everything contractual or financial the agent passed to a person on its own.

Before
·Read, sort and compose for every single request
·Escalation depends on experience and on how full the queue is
·No data on how the volume actually splits
After
→Confirm and send for the routine share
→Sensitive cases leave the automated path by rule, with a briefing attached
→Every decision logged, so the next automation step can be argued with numbers

Limitation. The demo works on desktop files. In a live setting the same workflow would have to be integrated into the ticket system or the CRM and write its proposal back as an internal note. The process stays the same: the agent proposes, the rules decide, a person sends.

Performance dialogue

Operations cockpit

The board for a weekly performance dialogue. The numbers which are cascaded from the production floor up to management reviews, here with six KPIs of an airline catering unit. It is filled from the figures that arrive by mail, marks whether a deviation is a single bad week or a repeated miss, and carries every agreed measure until it is closed.

Where this came from

Preparing the basis for performance dialogues showed me how much time goes into non value creating preparation work. This was a try to increase efficiency and quality throughout the whole process

Open the cockpit Synthetic data · 13 weeks · 6 KPIs · opens in this browser
Situation
Figures arrive as unstructured text in mails and are retyped by hand every week
What it does
Reads the mails, updates the values, sorts the deviations, proposes next steps and tracks the measures
Effect
Increased efficiency, quality and impact
01

The problem

  • The figures come in as sentences in mails from e.g. shift leads, quality and controlling. Every change requires manual editing
  • KPI values are reported without the story they're telling
  • Agreed measures come back only if somebody remembers them
02

How it works

Step 1
Collect
A scheduled run reads the KPI mails through the Gmail connector.
Step 2
Check
Each value is tested against a plausible range. Anything not found is marked.
Step 3
Interpret
Outliers, trends and target gaps are computed, reasons named in the mails carried into the summary.
Step 4
Track & send
Measures stay on the board with owner and due date until closed. After confirmation, board and weekly slide go to the distribution list.
03

What it changes

Before
·About an hour of manual data transfer per week
·Discussions fail to adress the relevant deviations.
·Isolated reasons retold from memory.
After
→Massive reduction of manual editing.
→Outliers and trends are separated, each with a proposed next step
→Context from the mails in the summary. Measure tracking only requires manual input only occasionally

Limitation. The public demo shows the board on synthetic data.

Personal app

Workout generator

The app guides you through 5 questions: equipment, level, focus, time, and a free text for injuries or anything else to consider. It then produces a session built for exactly that situation. Click on Go and it runs step by step with a timer, from warm-up to cool-down. Takes about thirty seconds to try.

Where this came from

My training slots are short and turn up unannounced often. By researching exercises and putting them together the first 10 minutes are gone.

Generate a workout Live version with Claude The live version needs a Claude account, the button on the left works without one
Situation
Short, unplanned workout slots. The deciding eats the time
What it does
Five answers in, a personalised guided session out
Effect
Training starts immediately, fits your personal needs and the minutes available
01

The problem

  • Available Equipment, focus and other circumstances change from week to week, so a plan needs to adapt
  • Choosing exercises, order and duration takes ten minutes of a thirty-minute window
  • Asking a chatbot produces something plausible but lacks important details and takes more time
02

How it works

Step 1
Guided menu
Equipment, level, focus, time plus a free field for injuries or sth. else.
Step 2
Session built
Only the equipment selected, the exact minutes available, every exercise explained.
Step 3
Guided run
Warm-up, work, rest and cool-down, one screen at a time with a timer.
03

What it changes

Before
·Ten minutes of deciding before anything starts
·Plans that ignore individual setting
·Sessions that overrun or end early
After
→Thirty seconds of input, then training
→Only the equipment at hand, injuries taken into account
→The time available, used exactly, guided by a timer
